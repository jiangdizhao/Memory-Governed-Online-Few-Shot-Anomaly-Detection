# Selection bias and identifiability

Legacy deterministic BEA can assign zero probability to low-utility regions. Their labels and normal score distribution cannot be recovered by inventing positive propensities after the fact. `STOCHASTIC_BEA_GROUP_SLOT_V1` selects a source group's one candidate ordinal uniformly from 1 through 5 using the seeded, domain-separated identity hash at the first observed view. It examines no future view. At that ordinal, `p=1` if B+N+D exceeds current cost and `p=0.05` otherwise; a separate identity-hashed draw selects the action. The receipt records both the 1/5 slot probability and p, and for the eligible specified view records the two-stage design probability `(1/5)*p`. Other views have no fabricated conditional gate or design probability in their receipt.

The hash design is reproducible pseudorandomization. Probability statements describe its stated randomization model; conditioning on a fixed seed yields a deterministic realized tape. The initial target is the randomized group-view population. Transport to full image-stream risk requires additional assumptions about view sampling, group frequency and label distributions.

Weights are the reciprocal of the actual recorded design probabilities. Their clipping at 50 reduces variance but introduces bias: an exploration event with pi=0.01 has raw weight 100 and operational weight 50. ESS is computed from clipped weights. Missing probability, a forged receipt, missing source identity, fewer than 12 groups, or ESS below 12 cannot be replaced by a nominal value. Diagnostics expose both weight arrays, ESS, per-scorer score-region coverage, executed/exploration counts, evidence ages and state versions, unavailable-label fraction, and suppression reasons.

The lagged score scale uses every prior observed GC score, without label filtering: the last 256 values, `max(1.4826*MAD, 0.02)`, or 0.02 for fewer than two prior values. It is an operational query scale, not a normal-class scale. The historical B/N/D component equations and correction scale are ported into a pure function. The price starts at 0.8 and updates after every actual action as `clip(c + 0.02*(rolling_executed_rate - 0.037), 0.05, 3.0)` over at most 256 image events. Historical D2's implementation imports the older 0.03 target into its price update even though D2 describes a 0.037 target; this new named variant deliberately uses the task's 0.037 target. The price is adaptive and does not guarantee a finite-horizon label count. No forced queries, catch-up, horizon quota or label-dependent lottery is used.

The memory and its admission history depend on earlier selected feedback. E rescores selected images in those adaptive states; clipping and adaptive state dependence prevent a claim that weighting alone estimates current-state population FPR without bias. No conformal, anytime, finite-sample coverage or population-risk guarantee is asserted. Independent review and a separately authorized empirical protocol are required.
